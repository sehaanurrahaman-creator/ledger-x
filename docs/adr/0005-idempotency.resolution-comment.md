**Resolved.** The decision is [`docs/adr/0005-idempotency.md`](https://github.com/sehaanurrahaman-creator/ledger-x/blob/main/docs/adr/0005-idempotency.md); the implementation is `dev.ledgerx.idempotency` (policy, receipt, refusals) plus the `postIdempotent` commit path in `dev.ledgerx.journal`, the `IDEMPOTENT_POSTING` record in the log, and the v2 state and timing sections in `dev.ledgerx.checkpoint`; the client-facing version of the contract is [`docs/idempotency.md`](https://github.com/sehaanurrahaman-creator/ledger-x/blob/main/docs/idempotency.md). The evidence is `./build.sh`: a fifteen-check idempotency contract (including the ticket's duplicate storm), a model-oracle property suite, the WAL and checkpoint contracts extended for the format versions, and the boundary harness re-armed over a keyed history.

**The contract, whole.** The identity of a request is `(merchant_id, idempotency_key)` — the key alone is nothing, and two merchants may use the same key without ever meeting. "The same body" means the entries exactly as sent, in the order sent: `SHA-256('P' ‖ encodePosted(transaction))`, the same bytes a plain `POSTED` record carries. The precedence is four branches, and the order is the contract: a **bound** key answers from the table before the candidate's merits are looked at — fingerprint differs → **409 Conflict**; fingerprint matches and age < retention → **replay**; fingerprint matches and age ≥ retention → **410 expired**. An **unbound** key's candidate is domain-validated first, and only a valid one binds. A replay returns the stored response flagged `replayed = true`, quoting the original record's LSN and the original capture instant, and appends nothing — a replay is a read. A reordered entry list is a different body and answers 409; that is stated rather than left to be discovered.

**One record, one commit.** The ticket's non-negotiable — idempotency records commit atomically with their transactions — is the design rather than a property of it: the binding and the posting are the *same bytes*, one `PostedIdempotently` event in one `IDEMPOTENT_POSTING` record, one append, one fsync, one ack. There is no index-update-before-or-after, no window between "the money moved" and "the key remembers", and no fourth state a crash could leave: the record is there (recovery re-binds it), torn (ADR 0003's rules cut it, and the retry posts fresh), or never written. The response is stored in the record itself, so replay re-reads it at the binding's LSN through the log's dense index. The one log that would falsify the theorem — one key bound in two records — is hand-crafted in the contract's eleventh check, and the open refuses it as `DOMAIN_REJECTED`: a duplicate binding is the exactly-once theorem's one way to be false, and the only correct response to a counterexample is to stop.

**The 409, and the 200-replay it refuses to be.** A same-key-different-body request is **409 Conflict, at any age**, carrying both fingerprints — the one the key is bound to and the one the request presented. This is the deliberate divergence from Stripe (research ticket, `docs/research/stripe-idempotency.md`), and the ticket asked for the danger played out: a payout client reuses Tuesday's key for Wednesday's payout, and Wednesday's amount is 250.01 rather than 250.00. Under a replay-instead-of-refuse rule the platform answers 200, returns Tuesday's response, credits 250.00, and creates no record of the divergence — the books balance, the client sees success, the cent is found at reconciliation at best. A 409 turns the same event into a loud, immediate failure with the diff in the error. 400 would say the wrong thing (the body is usually valid; the *key* is wrong), and the refusal outlives expiry because the binding is permanent. The full argument is ADR 0005 §3.

**Expiry and GC — standing question 3.** Nothing is GC'd. What expires is the stored response's *availability*: past retention (default 24 h, `IdempotencyPolicy(Clock, Duration)`, `SYSTEM` for production), a same-body retry is refused with `IdempotencyExpiredException` — 410, quoting the capture instant and the retention — never replayed, never re-executed. The binding itself is permanent for the life of the log, so a **different** body after expiry is still a 409: a key that ever meant one intent never means another, and the "GC'd key reused" scenario simply has no hole to fall into. The cost is stated: rows live as long as the log does, a handful of bytes per key, against an append-only file whose retention is the rotation ticket's.

**Clock skew — standing question 5.** The capture instant is read once, from the policy's injected clock, at the moment the record commits; replay consults nothing but the comparison. Age is floored at zero, so a backwards clock *extends* the replay window (harmless — a replay is a read), a forwards clock *shortens* it (loud — replays become 410s early), and no skew in either direction can turn a retry into a second posting, because the one dangerous direction — expiry as re-arming — is structurally absent. The wall clock is read in exactly one file, `IdempotencyPolicy.java`, enforced by the new lint rule 8 (self-tested like rules 6 and 7), so every capture instant is an input the tests control.

**The table's three homes, never a fourth.** The live index in memory (`InMemoryLedger`), the records in the log (the table's history *is* the log), and the checkpoint: binding rows — merchant, key, fingerprint, `responseLsn` — inside the hashed state section, and one capture instant per binding in a new timing section that is CRC'd but not hashed, so the state hash stays blind to timing and ADR 0004's five-way hash comparison keeps its meaning now that records carry wall-clock instants. The header word at +36 became `timingBytes`; the pairing is checked directly — a file whose timing count disagrees with its binding count is refused by the new `TIMING_MISMATCH`, and the contract builds that exact file to require the name. The segment version moved to 2: this build still reads v1 logs, refuses a v1 segment carrying the new record type as vocabulary damage however cleanly it parses, and refuses v3 at the header; the checkpoint format moved with it and v1 checkpoints still fold.

**The evidence.** `IdempotencyContract` — **15/15**, including the ticket's duplicate storm: 100 concurrent retries of one key on virtual threads → one posting, 99 replays, zero double-charges, one LSN; the recovery check (replays after a reopen are replays, aged keys refuse, conflicts survive); the checkpoint round trip (response re-materialized from the log, the instant aging exactly); and the hash-blind-to-timing check (two clocks, different logs, same hash). `IdempotencyProperties` — **3/3**, the ticket's theorem as a model oracle: every operation of 40 × 120 random cases predicted by the ADR's four branches and compared — outcome, LSN, capture instant, binding count, Σ balances — across random interleavings of retries, conflicts, expiries, reopens, checkpoints and skewed ticks (40 cases: `posted=1124 replayed=65 conflict=964 expired=126 rejected=769 reopened=331 checkpointed=235`). `WalContract` **20/20** with the version story; `CheckpointContract` **13/13** with the timing section; the boundary harness re-armed over a keyed history — every even operation posts under a fresh key, every keyed posting schedules a retry and a conflict, and stage kills arm on the golden run's own checkpoints — **71/71** cycles at 16 operations and **220/220** at 64. What the suites caught while being written is in ADR 0005 §8: a double-counted constant that only the recovery checks could see, a precedence the first oracle had backwards, and stage arms that could name an instant nothing happens.

**What it hands on.** To the payout-protocol ticket: the key's binding is the record, so "exactly-once across the bank boundary" starts from a table it can read. To the TLA⁺ ticket: ADR 0005 §4 is the prose the machine-checked exactly-once claim formalizes. To the public-API ticket: the 409/410 are exception types with structured accessors (`merchant()`, `key()`, `boundTo()`, `presented()`), and the `IdempotentReceipt` carries everything a `replayed` response header needs. To concurrency: keyed posts take the same commit lock as plain ones, and the storm is the evidence the serialization is not a bottleneck shape.
