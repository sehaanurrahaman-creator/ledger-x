**Resolved** — the ADR is [`docs/adr/0002-domain-model.md`](https://github.com/sehaanurrahaman-creator/ledger-x/blob/main/docs/adr/0002-domain-model.md), and the model it decides is implemented, tested and demonstrated in the same PR: `dev.ledgerx.domain`, a nine-check property suite, and `make demo`.

**The call: a transaction is n entries, not two.** At least two, Σ debits = Σ credits, every account already opened, no total or resulting balance allowed to overflow. The two-account transfer is a factory over that grammar, and that single fact answers the domain half of standing design-review question 4 — there is nothing to coordinate between two accounts, because there is only one unit of commit. What the cross-shard ticket is left with is a *storage* question, not a protocol one.

**Money is a signed `long` of integer minor units, one currency, exponent 2 as a constant, every operation overflow-checked.** `Math`'s class javadoc on jdk21u is the contract it leans on: "the methods whose names end with `Exact` throw an `ArithmeticException` when the results overflow". An amount that does not fit becomes a rejection — `OVERFLOWING_TOTALS` or `OVERFLOWING_BALANCE` — never a wrapped balance, because a wrapped balance breaks Σ balances = 0 *silently* and that is the one failure this project exists to make impossible. `BigDecimal` was rejected on allocation and scale-agreement grounds, `double` on the grounds `BigDecimal`'s own javadoc states better than any argument could (`new BigDecimal(0.1)` is "actually equal to 0.1000000000000000055511151231257827021181583404541015625"). The decision is enforced mechanically: `scripts/lint.sh` rule 7 fails the build on a `float` or `double` in the domain package, and it was verified both ways — injecting one into `Money.java` names the two offending lines, removing it passes.

**Three account kinds — `ASSET`, `LIABILITY`, `EQUITY` — each with a normal side, and none of them constrains anything.** Kind is metadata: no posting rule reads it, it cannot cause a rejection, and no invariant depends on it, which is why the property suite randomizes kinds freely and why adding `REVENUE` in a year is a one-line change. Revenue and expense are absent on purpose — they are equity sub-kinds whose purpose is to be closed into equity at the end of a period, and no downstream ticket asks for a period.

**Balances may go negative on every kind.** The ledger records; it does not judge. Three reasons: Σ balances = 0 constrains the sum and never an individual sign; a funds check needs ordering and retry facts that the concurrency and payout tickets own; and an acceptance rule that depends on *current* balances is poison at replay time, which is the destination. So an overdrawn asset is drift to detect (`AccountKind.isNaturalBalance` names it, `make demo` prints one) rather than a posting to refuse. The divergence from production ledger APIs is recorded rather than buried: Modern Treasury exposes `pending_balance`/`posted_balance`/`available_balance`, and ledger-x has one balance and no availability notion, because a pending/available split presupposes a two-phase posting lifecycle the charter's immutability rule rules out.

**Atomic rejection is validate-then-apply, and the property suite found a real bug in it on the first run.** The first version range-checked each account's *net* effect but applied entries *one at a time*, so a transaction that credited 232,390, debited 282,945 and credited 50,555 — netting to zero, validation passing — drove an already near-`Long.MIN_VALUE` balance out of range on its first entry and threw *after* the event had been appended. A partial mutation: exactly what the atomic-rejection rule forbids. Shrunk to four operations. The fix is structural: `validate` now returns the deltas it checked and `apply` consumes exactly those, so the two cannot disagree, and acceptance no longer depends on the order entries happen to be listed in.

**Rejection reasons are a closed enum in a fixed order — grammar, reference, arithmetic, money rule, representation limit — and the order is part of the contract.** A candidate can break two rules at once, and which reason comes back must not depend on which check was written first, because the idempotency ticket will map reasons onto statuses and a nondeterministic reason becomes a nondeterministic response body that then fails to match the stored one on replay. Six two-rule candidates pin the order, and the suite also asserts the enum's declaration order equals the order documented in the ADR.

**One sealed event log with two event kinds** (`AccountOpened`, `Posted`) rather than a journal plus a separate account list: one sequence space to fold instead of two lists needing a merge rule, with the sequence number being the position — which is what a WAL offset gives for free. A transaction carries **no id** (a stored one would be a second source of truth that could disagree with the log), **no timestamp** (a ledger-assigned clock would make replay non-deterministic, and byte-identical replay is the destination) and **no idempotency key** (that ticket's, and baking one in now would pre-empt it). Immutability is enforced at three levels, because one level would be an assertion rather than a proof: by type (records, 31 final fields across 13 types reflected over and confirmed, `List.copyOf` so a caller clearing its list cannot reach the journal — the suite does exactly that), by view (every accessor hands out a copy that throws on mutation, asserted by attempting it), and by observation (every event is rendered to a canonical string when first seen and re-rendered after every operation, since comparing an entry to itself cannot detect a field changed in place).

**The toolkit question: ADR 0001's dependency trigger did not fire, and the harness is hand-rolled.** ADR 0001 named JMH or JUnit as the trigger and wrote it down so the build's shape would be a decision rather than an accident; property-based tests in Java usually mean jqwik, which runs as a JUnit Platform engine. Two reasons, one of principle and one measured: honouring a trigger means not firing it early, and **a jqwik harness could not have been verified locally at all** — measured 2026-09-14, `repo1.maven.org` and `api.adoptium.net` both fail the TLS handshake from this sandbox while `pypi.org` and `registry.npmjs.org` answer. The harness is ~350 lines: SplitMix64 (implemented rather than taken from `java.util.Random`, because "reproduce with seed 1234" is only true if seed 1234 still produces that case after a JDK upgrade), delta-debugging shrinks, and failure reports carrying the seed, the minimal case and four stack frames. What was given up is tabulated in the ADR against what jqwik would have provided — 1,000 default tries, integrated shrinking, an edge-case database, a previous-run database — including the honest note that this shrinker finds a *local* minimum.

**What makes it more than a smoke test is the oracle.** Random operations alone cannot tell you a ledger is right, only that it is self-consistent: a ledger that computed every balance as zero would keep Σ balances = 0 forever. So `ModelLedger` is a second, deliberately slow and obvious ledger — `BigInteger` balances, straight-line validation transcribed from the same rules, no shared helper or state — and after every operation the suite asserts the two agree on which transactions are acceptable, why the unacceptable ones are unacceptable, and what every balance is afterwards. Plus a coverage guard, because a generator that quietly stopped producing invalid operations would leave every invariant trivially true: a campaign that never walked an acceptance, a construction refusal, all five rejection reasons, a four-entry transaction, a negative balance, a same-account-twice posting and a self-cancelling one fails with its counters in the message.

**Nine checks, all green, at three campaign sizes.** Default (`./build.sh`, 60 cases × 200 operations): `PASS 9/9 property checks — 127 random cases`, 12,000 campaign operations, 5,838 accepted / 3,706 rejected / 844 refused at construction, widest transaction 12 entries, reasons `TOO_FEW_ENTRIES=492 UNKNOWN_ACCOUNT=785 OVERFLOWING_TOTALS=457 UNBALANCED=1137 OVERFLOWING_BALANCE=835`, in ~3 s. `make campaign` (150 × 400 = 60,000 operations): `PASS 9/9`, 30,344 accepted / 17,946 rejected / 4,153 refused, ~22 s. By hand at 400 × 600 = 240,000 operations: `PASS 9/9`, ~141 s. Cost is superlinear in operations per case because every operation re-audits and re-renders the whole journal — the price of checking after *every* step instead of at the end, and the right trade for a prototype whose only job is to be correct. Those three timings are the first real data points for the map's CI-budget fog.

**`make demo` posts a two-account transfer and prints the balances and the ledger**, and it demonstrates by asserting: nine events (four accounts, funding out of equity, the transfer of 123.45, a card payment that makes the platform owe a merchant, a three-entry transaction settling one payable from two accounts, a refund that drives an asset to −26.55 and is accepted anyway), two transactions refused atomically with the event count and every balance compared before and after, the whole journal in append order, every balance on the one debit-positive axis with its kind and whether that balance is natural for the kind, and the audit. Σ balances = 0 is checked before it is printed and the program exits non-zero if any claim is untrue, so a demo that lies cannot get through CI. It reads no clock, file, socket or random generator, so it prints the same bytes every run.

**Also in this change, because the build had to grow to carry it:** `./build.sh` gains `compile`, `test`, `demo` and `campaign` subcommands with both test mains publishing their verdict as a CI annotation; campaign size and seed are environment variables (`LEDGER_X_TRIALS`, `LEDGER_X_OPERATIONS`, `LEDGER_X_SEED`) so a failing seed replays without an edit; `-Dstdout.encoding=UTF-8` on every test run, because the JVM's console-encoding fallback prints `?` for Σ in the annotations that get read back from the API; `scripts/lint.sh` now lints untracked files as well as the index (a new file was invisible to it until somebody remembered to `git add`), counts columns as bytes under `LC_ALL=C` (awk counts characters in a UTF-8 locale, so a javadoc line with an em dash measured differently here than in CI — a lint that goes red for no reason), and self-tests both ADR rules on every run; `scripts/bootstrap-toolchain.sh` installs `java`/`javac` shims so `./build.sh` itself runs where no JDK is installable; and CI runs `./build.sh`, then `make demo`, then `make campaign`.

**Stated plainly, what was not verified here.** The local compiler is ECJ, not javac, so `-Xlint:all -Werror` remains CI's call and CI's javac run is the check of record. One divergence between the two was found and designed around: ECJ ignores `@SuppressWarnings("unchecked")` once `-err:+unchecked` promotes the warning, so a cast javac accepts does not compile locally — nothing in `src/` depends on a suppression, and the audit check's reflection goes through `Method.invoke` instead.

**Fog graduated.** One new decision, genuinely new: *does an account belong to a merchant, and where does that scope live?* `AccountId` is unscoped, the idempotency key is `(merchant_id, idempotency_key)`, and both cannot stay as they are — either the scope becomes part of the account's identity or something has to enforce that a transaction never mixes merchants' accounts. Blocked by this ticket, blocking the idempotency one. Five other findings feed existing tickets rather than creating new ones and are written into the ADR for them: the entry-count ceiling and record encoding to the WAL ticket, canonicalization to the checkpoint ticket, the missing pending state to the payout ticket, the natural-balance vocabulary to the reconciliation ticket, and the now-half-cleared benchmark workload shape to the metrics ticket.
